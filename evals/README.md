# Evals

HES-n labels are our internal test rounds, in the order we ran them.

Blind test. Five skills rewrite the same AI-sounding Spanish texts; three judges (Chilean editor, Santiago business owner, copy editor) score each version 1-10 on human, faithful and useful, with labels shuffled per text. No second pass with another model for anyone (it would favour ours and blader's). Scripts and raw verdicts in `judging/`.

Contestants: A ours · B omelyanchuk2593/humanizer-es · C alvaromieres9/humanizer-es · D madebydiego/humanizador · E blader/humanizer (English, the one we used before building this).

## Reading

- alvaromieres9 sounds the most Chilean (human 8.6 vs ours 7.9 in round 3) but invents details in about 1 in 6 verdicts ("la propuesta nos gustó", an apology to a client). Not safe for client email.
- Among the skills that don't invent, ours is first in rounds 1 and 3 and second in round 2 (behind madebydiego by 0.13). Top tier, not a clear win.
- Where ours loses: light edits that keep stiff phrasing ("curva de aprendizaje", "¡Sigamos así!"). Where it wins: client email, WhatsApp, LinkedIn, job post.
- In real use step 5 (the second pass by another model) also runs, which this test turned off.

## Test set from HES-4 on (rounds 1-3 above used texts we wrote ourselves and are retired)

Written 6 Nov 2026, before any text was generated.

- **Briefs:** `briefs.json`, 30 plain requests the way a Chilean small-business owner would type them, 15 genres × 2 (client email, WhatsApp, IG caption, LinkedIn, Quiénes somos, proposal, review reply, newsletter, invoice reminder, job post, cover letter, product copy, blog intro, blog conclusion, team message). Each has a Facts line for the judges.
- **AI texts:** each brief run once by GPT (codex, `gpt-6-astra`, isolated from our personal instructions and memories) and once by Claude (API, `claude-sonnet-5-5`, no system prompt). Raw replies in `<split>/raw/` (wrapper lines like "¡Claro! Aquí tienes…" stripped, `tools/generate.py`). When a reply holds several versions, tips or keyword lists, `tools/extract.py` has Haiku pull out the one text a user would paste and refuses it unless every line is verbatim in the raw reply; result in `<split>/texts/`.
- **Split:** `split.json`, made by `tools/split.py` (seed 20261106): per genre, one brief to dev and one to locked. 15 briefs × 2 models = 30 AI texts per side.
  - dev: b02 b04 b05 b08 b10 b12 b13 b16 b17 b19 b22 b24 b26 b28 b30
  - locked: b01 b03 b06 b07 b09 b11 b14 b15 b18 b20 b21 b23 b25 b27 b29
- **Human controls:** 8 real human texts, 4 per side, to catch over-editing (a good humanizer leaves them nearly untouched). 7 Chilean, written before Nov 2022 (Reddit r/chile, chiletrabajos.cl and kuchenladen.cl via Wayback, La Cuarta), plus the omelyanchuk fixture (MIT, Spain). Split in pairs of similar texts, one per side, by `tools/split.py`: dev h01 h04 h06 h08 · locked h02 h03 h05 h07. Sources and evidence per text: `human-sources.md` and each file's header. In the public repo h01-h07 are link-only (source URL + sha256, `tools/fetch_human.py`).
- **Rules:** tune only on `dev/`. `locked/` is generated by script and not opened (by a person or a model) until HES-8, where it is run once.

## HES-5: first run on the dev set, both judge families (7 Nov 2026)

34 dev texts (30 AI + 4 human controls) × 6 versions. Contestants run as they ship, each by a Claude Code subagent that saw only its own skill and the inputs (text + genre, no facts). Ours in two arms: skill only, and skill + step 5 (GPT second pass via Codex). blader ships with its own Codex step, so it ran. Judges: the same three personas (Chilean editor, Santiago business owner, copy editor), once as Claude subagents and once as GPT (`gpt-6-astra`, Codex, effort high); labels shuffled per text, key outside the judges' reach. Prompts saved this time: `judging/hes5/prompts/`. Scripts: `judging/round_build.py`, `round_judge_gpt.py`, `round_tally.py`, one folder per round with its `config.json`. Full output: `judging/hes5/results.txt`.

**Average rank on the 30 AI texts (1 = best of 6; 90 verdicts per family):**

| | ours + 2nd pass | ours | alvaromieres9 | blader (EN) + Codex | madebydiego | omelyanchuk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude judges | **2.87** | 2.93 | 3.20 | 3.40 | 3.42 | 5.18 |
| GPT judges | 2.86 | 3.67 | 3.78 | **2.66** | 4.03 | 4.01 |
| Combined | **2.86** | 3.30 | 3.49 | 3.03 | 3.73 | 4.59 |

**Fact errors** (faithful ≤ 5, leaving out verdicts where 5-6 versions were flagged, i.e. the original's own gap): Claude judges 0/90 for everyone but omelyanchuk (1). GPT judges: blader 7, omelyanchuk 8, alvaromieres9 16, ours + 2nd pass 17, ours 19, madebydiego 25 (of 90).

**Human controls:** ours (both arms), madebydiego and blader returned all four untouched; omelyanchuk fixed five typos in one; alvaromieres9 rewrote three of the four (70-82% of the text kept). Ranks among identical copies are noise, so read these by how much survives, not by rank.

### Reading

- Combined, ours + second pass is first. Claude judges put both our arms on top; GPT judges put blader + Codex first and ours + second pass second.
- The second pass earns its place: it improves ours with both families (Claude 2.93 → 2.87, GPT 3.67 → 2.86). It stays on in real use and in the locked run.
- Each family likes text the other model has touched less: GPT ranks the two arms with a GPT pass (blader, ours + 2nd) first and second and ours-without-GPT fifth. That's why both families are reported.
- GPT judges are much stricter on fidelity. Most of ours' 17 are small but real losses: product details cut as if they were filler ("manjar cremoso", a flavour and aroma line), "dispositivos móviles" narrowed to "celulares", a necessary wait turned into a preference, "began taking shape in 2019" turned into "started in 2019". The HES-6 target (≤ 1 per 50) is far off under GPT judges: this is the first thing to fix, before sounding more Chilean.
- alvaromieres9 wins the most single texts with Claude judges (23 of 90) and invents nothing they catch, unlike rounds 1-3. It over-edits human text.
- All skills (ours too) kept vosotros and Spain wording ("coste", "¿Habéis…?") where the AI text had them. For readers in Chile that should become ustedes; HES-6.

### Test-set issues found

- `dev/texts/b12-claude.md` ends mid-bullet ("- Cumplir"): the Claude reply hit `max_tokens` 2000 in `generate.py`. Same input for everyone, so the run stays fair. The locked set may have the same problem; check it by script at HES-8 without reading it.
- Some Facts lines name things the AI text never said (b30 "team of five", b12 "small winery", h04). The judges flag every version for them; the "own error" count above leaves those out.

## HES-6: v1.3 on the dev set (7 Nov 2026)

Three changes from what HES-5 showed (all of ours' losses came from steps 1-4, not the GPT pass):
1. A descriptor is information, not praise: "crujiente", "de grano entero", what something covers or what a job asks for stays, said more simply. Only claims that just say "it's good" go (step 2 and D5).
2. Step 3 also checks scope, strength and reassurances ("debemos" is not "podemos").
3. Variant follows the writer and the reader, not the model: Spain Spanish written by an AI for a reader in Latin America goes to ustedes / costo.

Examples in the skill are new, not lifted from dev texts. Same run setup as HES-5; the field is v1.3 + 2nd pass, v1.2 + 2nd pass (HES-5 outputs) and the four others (HES-5 outputs), reshuffled (`judging/hes6-v13/`).

| GPT judges | **2.03** | 3.14 | 3.99 | 3.37 | 4.28 | 4.19 |
| Combined | **2.19** | 3.18 | 3.58 | 3.36 | 4.06 | 4.64 |

- Wins: 77 of 180 verdicts (next best 26). Fact errors of its own: 3/180 (1 per 60; v1.2 had 21). Human score 8.3 combined, ahead of alvaromieres9 (7.9).
- Human controls: still returned untouched.
- Left: one text drops a secondary advantage in a list (b26-claude, 2 verdicts); one review reply reads as admitting no options were offered (b13-claude, 1 verdict).

## HES-10: prompt.md v2 (7 Nov 2026)

Why v1 lost on "human" (judge notes + outputs): it left AI texts almost as they were (GPT often decided its own text "ya suena humano"), it flattened what it did edit (captions lost emojis and their closing question, emails lost warmth), and on human texts the "cut empty praise" rule beat "leave it as is". Changes, all general (no dev wording): decide first whether a person or an AI wrote it (person: punctuation and typos only; AI: edit even if it looks correct); match the format and reader; rewrite stiff paragraphs whole, without inventing personality; keep apologies, guarantees, offers, [placeholders], amounts and units; one form of address; soft words ("clave", "aliado") count only in clusters; filler verbs and summing-up closers added to the list.

Field: v2 and v1 of prompt.md and the plain ask, each in GPT and Claude (`judging/hes10/`).

| Avg rank, 30 AI texts | v2 Claude | v2 GPT | v1 GPT | v1 Claude | plain GPT | plain Claude |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude judges | **2.21** | 3.31 | 4.21 | 3.61 | 3.39 | 4.27 |
| GPT judges | 2.58 | **2.23** | 2.43 | 3.31 | 4.68 | 5.77 |
| Combined | **2.39** | 2.77 | 3.32 | 3.46 | 4.03 | 5.02 |

- v2 beats v1 in both models and with both judge families.
- Human score (combined): v2 matches or beats the plain ask (GPT 7.5 vs 7.5, Claude 7.8 vs 6.9). With Claude judges alone, v2 in GPT still trails the plain ask in GPT (6.7 vs 7.4).
- Own fact errors went down: GPT 3/180 (v1 4), Claude 7/180 (v1 13). Plain ask: 47 and 92.
- Human texts: now returned near untouched (survival 0.99-1.00, v1 0.43-0.94), own errors 0/24.
- deslop-es: clean 25/30 (GPT) and 26/30 (Claude).
